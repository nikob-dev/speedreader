THE READING ROOM

How Your Eyes Actually Read a Page

Essay · 6 min read

When you read a line of text, your eyes do not glide smoothly across it. They jump in short hops called saccades, pausing for a fraction of a second on each word before leaping to the next one.

Those jumps take time, and every so often your eyes slip backwards to re-read a phrase they already passed. Rapid serial visual presentation removes the jumps entirely by bringing each word to the same spot on the screen.

With the movement gone, attention can stay on meaning instead of on navigation, which is why many readers find they can comfortably go faster than they expected.
